I am an [HFSP long-term postdoctoral research fellow](https://www.hfsp.org/) at the [Social Neuroscience Lab](https://psnlab.princeton.edu/) in Princeton University. I am interested in human communication and social cognition. My current research employs neuroimaging and two-person approaches to study why we enjoy and engage in social interactions. You can read more about my research [here](https://elidom.github.io/home/research/).

### My background
I grew up in Guadalajara, Mexico — home to mariachi, tequila, and one of my favorite filmmakers, Guillermo del Toro. I earned a **Licenciatura in Psychology** from the University of Guadalajara and a **Master of Science** from the Institute of Neurobiology (INB) at the National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM). Along the way, I also worked as a language instructor (Spanish, English, and German) in diverse corporate and academic settings, an experience that sparked my passion for teaching.

Between 2021 and 2026, I completed my PhD at McGill University’s School of Communication Sciences and Disorders, with the complementary Language Acquisition Program. My research examined what makes social speech engaging and how people coordinate their behavior and brain activity during good (and less good) phatic conversations.

### Teaching

I'm also very passionate about teaching and have served twice as a Teaching Assistant for the course **Research Methods and Laboratory in Human Perception** (PSYC 353) at McGill. There, I was responsible for the practical component of the course, where I taught students how to use MATLAB to code perceptual experiments and test psychophysical hypotheses. By the end of the course, students were able to apply their knowledge to program their own experiments. I have also been guest lecturer for **Introduction to Speech Science** (LING 210) and **Neurolinguistics** (SCSD 638) at McGill University. I have also co-supervised an honors research project and multiple research assistants.
